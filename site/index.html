REPEATABLE VALIDATION
Where does detection happen?

Your SIEM evaluates the telemetry and runs the detection logic, including any correlation rules. MayaTrail receives the fired alerts you forward; it does not run those rules against your raw logs. It matches received alerts to the exercised techniques and shows the supporting evidence and detection gaps.

A technique with no matching alert needs investigation: the cause could be a missing rule, missing telemetry or an alert-delivery problem. MayaTrail provides suggested detection content and response guidance. Your team reviews and applies changes in the SIEM, then repeats the exercise to validate the result.

Detection gaps

The gap is the finding. Everything else supports it.

For each exercised technique, inspect the matching alerts, any detection gaps and anything left unmeasured.

Fired

Your detection saw it

Inspect the matching alert and whether it identifies an exact rule or supports detection of the technique. Keep the rule, and retest it later.

Detection gap

No matching alert observed

The technique ran, but MayaTrail received no matching alert. Investigate the detection rule, telemetry and alert delivery to establish the cause.

Fair questions

Including the ones that are awkward for us.

What data does the alert integration use?

The validation workflow receives detection alerts forwarded by your SIEM, rather than bulk raw logs. Alerts can still contain sensitive fields. Before connecting, agree which fields are sent, who can access them, retention and deletion requirements, and the permissions used for the exercise.

Do we need a working SIEM to get started?

No SIEM is needed for a conversation or guided product walkthrough. To validate your own detections, you need the relevant telemetry in your SIEM and a verified alert-forwarding integration. An emulation without that integration can demonstrate execution, but cannot prove that your detections fired.

Is there a free hosted tier?

There is no public free hosted tier. Start with a guided demo, then discuss exploratory access and lab availability. Customer-connected exercises require agreed cloud access and SIEM setup; browsing and the initial demo do not.

Which clouds and platforms does it cover?

We started with AWS and Kubernetes, and went deep on both. For AWS that includes incident-based campaigns, research-based scenarios and single-technique emulations. For Kubernetes it is emulations of cluster-level attacks. Azure and GCP are next on the roadmap. Emulations package the attack behavior and supporting content, with infrastructure where the scenario requires it. Scope and prerequisites are stated in the product.

Do you do anything destructive to our environment?

Emulations act on disposable resources provisioned for the exercise, and some scenarios intentionally change or destroy those resources. Use a dedicated test account and review the scenario permissions, boundaries and costs first. Cleanup is scheduled; confirm that it completes and investigate any remaining resources.

What if the assessment shows our coverage is fine?

Then you have evidence for the techniques and environment assessed. Decide whether recurring exercises, detection changes or response practice justify a subscription. A successful exercise does not establish complete coverage.

What can you not test for?

Each campaign declares its techniques and scope. Lab evidence cannot establish coverage for untested techniques, every production attack path or the quality of a human investigation. Without a working alert integration, detection outcomes remain unvalidated. Review unsupported and unmeasured items alongside the findings.
