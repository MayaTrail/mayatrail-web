How the billing actually works

No seats, no per-run metering, no surprises on the invoice. Here is the whole model.

Everything runs in your account, not ours

We provision the disposable environment inside your AWS account and attack it from there, well away from production. That is not a deployment detail. It is the only way the activity reaches your CloudTrail and therefore your SIEM. An emulation run in our cloud would prove nothing about your detections.

You are billed per connected AWS account

Not per seat and not per user. Detection teams are small and we would rather three more of your engineers used it than not. Add an account and it is $2,000–3,000 more.

Re-running is free and always will be

Re-running is the behaviour that proves the product works, so charging for it would be charging you for the thing we want you to do. Run a campaign fifty times in a week if that is what closing the gap takes.

The AWS spend is yours, and it is small

Emulations run in your own account, so the infrastructure appears on your bill rather than ours. Our own estimates put a campaign in the order of cents per hour, and every environment carries a TTL and destroys itself.

We never hold long-lived keys

Access is a scoped cross-account IAM role, assumed through STS at run time. You can revoke it in one action, and nothing we store can be used to get back in.

Fair questions

Including the ones that are awkward for us.

Why pay, when the core is open source and so is everything else in this space?

Because running the attack is the easy half. Stratus, CloudGoat, Pacu and Atomic Red Team will all execute technique against your cloud for free, and so will our own core. None of them tell you whether your detections noticed. What you are paying for is the verdict (fired, silent, or no logs at all, measured against your real CloudTrail) and the loop that closes the gap and then checks it stayed closed.

Is there a free hosted tier?

No. A hosted run assumes a role in your AWS account and provisions real infrastructure; that is not something we are willing to hand out unattended to people we have never spoken to. Self-host the open source core for free, or talk to us.

Which clouds do you support?

AWS. That is the honest answer today. The campaigns, the detections and the coverage model are all built on CloudTrail. We would rather be genuinely good at one cloud than shallow across three.

Do you do anything destructive to our environment?

No. Campaigns run against a disposable environment we provision from code inside your account, not against your production estate, and every environment carries a TTL and is destroyed on schedule. The point is to generate the telemetry your detections should catch, not to compromise you.

What if the assessment shows our coverage is fine?

Then you have evidence you did not have before, and you should not buy the subscription yet. We would rather you came back when a re-test matters than pay for a year of confirming something you already know.

What can you not test for?

Every campaign declares the techniques it executes, and the product reports any of them that no detection rule covers, separately from rules that ran and stayed quiet, because those are our gap and not yours. You see that list in the product and in the assessment report. We would rather show you the hole than round the number up.
